Neil Armstrong Spacesuit — 3D model attribution
================================================

Object
------
Neil Armstrong Space Suit (Apollo A7-L pressure garment assembly), worn by
Neil Armstrong during the Apollo 11 mission, July 1969.

Canonical source (Smithsonian Institution)
------------------------------------------
https://3d.si.edu/object/3d/d8c63ba6-4ebc-11ea-b77f-2e728ce88125

Provided by
-----------
Smithsonian Digitization Programs Office (DPO) and the
National Air and Space Museum (NASM), Smithsonian Institution.

The suit was digitized by the Smithsonian using laser-arm scanning,
structured-light scanning, photogrammetry and CT scanning.

Retrieved from
--------------
This project obtained the low-resolution GLB from the documented public
mirror maintained by Google's <model-viewer> project:

  https://modelviewer.dev/shared-assets/models/NeilArmstrong.glb

That mirror's own attribution file states, verbatim:

  "Neil Armstrong Space Suit provided by the Smithsonian Digitization
   Programs Office and the National Air and Space Museum;
   Usage Conditions Apply."

  https://github.com/google/model-viewer/blob/master/packages/shared-assets/ATTRIBUTIONS.md

The object identifier in that attribution
(d8c63ba6-4ebc-11ea-b77f-2e728ce88125) matches the canonical Smithsonian
object page above, confirming this is the original Smithsonian scan and
not a substitute or a reconstruction.

Important modelling limitation
------------------------------
This scan is a SINGLE MERGED MESH. It has no separable internal assemblies.
Therefore LunaNav's Internal Inspection, Exploded Assembly, Cross-Section
and layer-dissection modes use a clearly-labelled generic EVA reference
architecture instead. Those views are NOT the internal construction of
Armstrong's A7-L, and are NOT NASA/Axiom CAD.

This artifact is an Apollo A7-L suit. It is NOT an AxEMU, not a NASA Z-2,
and not an ISS EMU. Those are different architectures.
